## The STAR Method

Behavioral questions ("tell me about a time you disagreed with your manager") are best answered with STAR: Situation sets context in one or two sentences, Task states what you were responsible for, Action describes what you specifically did — using "I" rather than "we" — and Result closes with a measurable outcome and what you learned. The most common failure is spending the whole answer on Situation; the Action and Result must take at least half the response.

## Questions Interviewers Use to Go Deeper

Follow-ups separate practiced stories from real ones: "what would you do differently?", "what did your manager think afterward?", "how did you convince the person who disagreed?", "what would have happened if you hadn't intervened?" Good candidates admit a real mistake, describe the correction, and name the habit they changed afterward — perfection stories signal fabrication. For conflict questions, interviewers listen for collaboration over combat: did the candidate seek shared context, escalate appropriately, and preserve the relationship while still shipping?

## Handling Pressure and Failure

Questions about missed deadlines or production incidents reveal ownership. A credible answer covers detection (how the problem surfaced), containment (stop the bleeding first), communication (who was told, how often), root cause (blameless analysis), and prevention (the change that makes recurrence unlikely). Candidates who blame colleagues, hide behind process, or skip the prevention step score poorly; those who describe a blameless postmortem, a runbook, or an alerting improvement score well.
